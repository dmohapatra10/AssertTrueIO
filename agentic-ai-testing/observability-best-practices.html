Observability Best Practices

Beginner 9 min read

Prerequisites: Debugging with Observability Data

What is this?

Seven pages, each building one genuine piece of observability infrastructure. This closing page ties them into a coherent checklist — not new material, but the honest synthesis of what this chapter actually established, and why each piece earns its place.

A Genuine, Traceable Checklist

☐ Structured, not text — every log entry uses queryable
  fields, passing the "one-line query" test (Structured Logging)

☐ Traced continuously, not just for tests — every real
  production run captures its full reasoning chain, not just
  deliberately constructed test scenarios (Tracing Agent
  Decisions)

☐ Testing-system health tracked separately from application
  health — a degraded API or MCP connection is never confused
  with a genuine application defect (Monitoring AI Test
  Systems)

☐ Alerts require sustained patterns or statistical
  significance, never a single data point — honoring Chapter
  8's own alert-fatigue warning (Alerting on AI Test Failures)

☐ Dashboards structured by category, never one blended score
  — application health, system health, and reasoning quality
  stay visually distinct (Dashboards for QA Automation)

☐ Debugging starts from standing data when available, reserving
  ad-hoc evidence gathering for genuinely novel questions
  (Debugging with Observability Data)

Why This Checklist Is Genuinely Traceable, Not a Generic List

EVERY item ABOVE points BACK to a SPECIFIC page, a SPECIFIC
WORKED example, and a SPECIFIC reason it MATTERS — this ISN'T
a GENERIC "best PRACTICES" list COPIED from SOMEWHERE else. IT
is THIS chapter's OWN argument, COMPRESSED. If ANY item on
THIS list feels UNFAMILIAR, that's a SIGNAL to REVISIT the
SPECIFIC page it CAME from, not to TRUST the CHECKLIST blindly.

🧠 Think About It

Why does this page deliberately avoid introducing any genuinely new technique, closing the chapter purely through synthesis? A checklist that introduces new material at the very end risks feeling disconnected from everything that came before — exactly the opposite of what a closing page should do. By tracing every item explicitly back to its source page, this page reinforces that observability isn't a collection of independent tips, but one coherent practice where each piece depends on and reinforces the others, mirroring how Chapters 5, 6, 7, 10, and 12 all closed their own chapters through synthesis rather than new content.

⚠️ Common Mistake

Treating a best-practices checklist like this one as a substitute for understanding why each item matters, rather than a compressed reminder of arguments already made in depth. A checklist item without its underlying reasoning is easy to follow mechanically and just as easy to abandon under pressure.

💡 Quick Tip

When onboarding a new team member to an existing observability practice, walking through this chapter's pages in order — rather than starting with this checklist alone — builds the same genuine understanding this page assumes, rather than handing them rules without the reasoning behind them.

Best Practices

  • Treat this checklist as a compressed reminder of arguments made in depth elsewhere in this chapter, not a standalone set of rules.
  • Revisit a specific earlier page whenever a checklist item feels unfamiliar, rather than trusting the compressed version blindly.
  • Introduce new team members to the reasoning behind each practice, not just the checklist itself.

🎯 Key Takeaway

  • This chapter's observability practice is one coherent whole — structured data, continuous tracing, separated health categories, calibrated alerts, category-based dashboards, and standing-data debugging — not a collection of independent tips.
  • Every checklist item traces back to a specific page and worked example, making the reasoning behind it recoverable, not just the rule itself.
  • This closing page continues the same synthesis-not-new-material pattern this course has used to close every major chapter since Chapter 5.
