Why This Chapter Opens With Fundamentals, Not AI

This chapter's first page is deliberately about Playwright's own locator best practices — no AI involved at all. Locator healing exists to handle a real, unavoidable problem: even well-written, stable locators eventually break when an application genuinely changes. It's a safety net for good locators, not a substitute for them — a distinction this chapter's closing page returns to directly.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating AI locator suggestions as a shortcut past writing good locators in the first place. This chapter's opening and closing pages both push back on this directly.
  • Accepting the first AI-suggested locator without generating alternatives or validating any of them. This chapter's middle pages exist specifically because a single, unvalidated suggestion carries real, genuine risk.

Best Practices

  • Read this chapter in order — each page's output feeds the next, ending in a real, assembled healing pipeline.
  • Keep this chapter's architecture diagram in mind — it's the same "generate several, validate, pick the best" pattern that shows up again in later chapters.

Key Takeaways

  • Locator healing is a genuine safety net for stable locators that eventually break — never a substitute for writing them well in the first place.
  • Multiple candidates, ranked and validated before selection, is what makes this chapter's approach meaningfully safer than trusting one AI suggestion.
  • This chapter closes by being explicit about where AI locator suggestions genuinely don't belong, not just where they help.
