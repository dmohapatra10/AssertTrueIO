Why It Matters — Only One Box Is Genuinely New

Look carefully at the mapping above: five of six boxes point directly to functions this track already built and explained in real depth. Only the Evaluator is genuinely new — deciding whether an objective has actually been achieved is a real, different question from anything earlier chapters needed to answer, since Chapter 5's instruction had explicit steps that simply "finished," and Chapter 13's exploration ran until a limit was hit, never asking "is the objective met" as its own, explicit question.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming every box in this diagram requires new code. This page's own mapping shows five of six are direct reuses — recognizing this is more valuable than expecting to write everything from scratch.
  • Treating Recovery as always invoked, every step. The example above shows it's conditional — only invoked when something genuinely breaks, not part of every single iteration.

Best Practices

  • Focus your genuine attention on the Evaluator, since it's the one box this chapter actually builds from scratch.
