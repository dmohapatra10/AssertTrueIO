What AI Can and Cannot Do

Prerequisites: AI-Assisted vs AI-Driven vs Autonomous Testing

What You'll Learn

Introduction

This tutorial's entire approach depends on being honest about both sides of this page's title — genuine capability and genuine limitation. Overselling AI's capability leads to real, costly mistakes later; ignoring its limitations leads to the same place. This page is the honest baseline every later chapter builds on.

What AI Can Genuinely Do

Test generation      — turning a requirement into a genuinely
                        broader set of scenarios than most
                        humans write by hand (Chapter 4)
Test maintenance      — suggesting locator fixes when a real
                         page changes (Chapter 6, 11)
Failure analysis        — classifying WHY a test failed, not
                           just reporting THAT it failed (Ch 9)
Test data generation      — realistic, varied, context-aware
                             values (Chapter 8)
Exploratory testing          — finding bugs nobody wrote a test
                                for yet (Chapter 13)
Locator assistance              — suggesting stable, ranked
                                   candidates (Chapter 6)
Visual analysis                    — recognizing UI changes a
                                      pixel-diff tool would
                                      miss or over-flag (Ch 17)

What AI Genuinely Cannot Do Reliably

Hallucinations         — AI can state something confidently
                          and fluently that's simply WRONG —
                          a locator that doesn't exist, a root
                          cause that isn't the real one
Incorrect assumptions    — AI reasons from PATTERNS, not
                            genuine understanding of YOUR
                            specific application's real intent
False positives             — flagging something as broken
                               that's actually fine
False negatives                — missing something that's
                                  genuinely broken
Cost, latency, and security      — every AI call has a REAL
                                    cost, REAL delay, and a
                                    REAL security surface
                                    (Chapter 18, 19 in full)

Example — Hallucination, Concretely

Asked to suggest a locator for ShopSmart's "Add to Cart" button, an AI model might confidently suggest page.getByTestId('add-to-cart-btn') — a perfectly reasonable-sounding guess, and one that simply doesn't exist on the real page, because ShopSmart actually uses page.getByRole('button', { name: 'Add to Cart' }). The suggestion sounds completely plausible. It's also wrong. This is exactly why Chapter 6 insists on validating every AI locator suggestion against the real page before trusting it — never accepting a suggestion on faith.

Common Mistakes

  • Trusting AI output because it sounds confident. Confidence and correctness are genuinely unrelated — a hallucinated locator sounds exactly as certain as a correct one.
  • Ignoring cost and latency until they become a real problem. Chapter 18 covers this in depth specifically because teams who ignore it early end up rebuilding their AI integration later.

Best Practices

  • Validate AI output against ground truth wherever ground truth is available — the real page, the real API response, the real test history.
  • Keep this page's two lists in mind as a genuine checklist whenever you're deciding how much to trust a new AI-powered technique.

Hands-On Exercise

For each capability in this page's first list, write one sentence describing what "validating the AI's output" would concretely look like. If you can't describe a concrete validation step, that's worth noticing — it may mean the capability needs more scaffolding before it's trustworthy in your own context.

Key Takeaways

  • AI brings genuine, real capability to testing — generation, maintenance, failure analysis, exploration — none of it magic, all of it useful.
  • AI also carries genuine, real limitations — hallucination, false positives and negatives, cost, latency, security risk — that never fully disappear.
  • This tutorial's approach throughout is validating AI output rather than trusting it, exactly because both lists on this page are genuinely true at once.
