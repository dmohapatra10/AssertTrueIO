🧠 Think About It

Why does this page treat governance and compliance as two sides of ONE framework, rather than two separate concerns needing separate frameworks? An organization's internal policy choices (governance) need to operate within whatever external requirements (compliance) actually apply — a governance decision that conflicts with a genuine regulatory requirement isn't a real choice at all. Treating them as one integrated framework, where compliance sets the floor and governance can build stricter policy on top, avoids the genuine risk of an internal policy that looks reasonable in isolation but conflicts with an external, binding requirement.

⚠️ Common Mistake

Treating this chapter's six earlier pages as a complete, sufficient practice without checking whether any external compliance requirements apply to a specific organization or industry. Good internal practice and legal compliance are related but genuinely different bars — the first doesn't automatically satisfy the second.

💡 Quick Tip

When a specific compliance requirement applies (GDPR, SOC 2, industry-specific rules), the genuinely useful first step is mapping each requirement directly onto this chapter's six categories — data privacy requirements map to page 3, audit requirements to page 5, and so on — rather than treating compliance as an entirely separate, parallel effort.

Best Practices

  • Verify which external compliance requirements genuinely apply to a specific organization, rather than assuming good internal practice is automatically sufficient.
  • Map specific compliance requirements onto this chapter's established categories, rather than treating compliance as a separate, parallel effort.
