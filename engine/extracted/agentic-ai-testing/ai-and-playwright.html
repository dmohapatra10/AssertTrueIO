The Running Example for This Chapter

ShopEasy's product SEARCH and PRODUCT DETAIL flow:

- A search box with autocomplete suggestions
- A results grid with filtering (price range, category) and
  sorting (price, relevance, newest)
- A product detail page with images, price, stock status,
  and an "Add to Cart" button that's disabled when out of stock

This flow genuinely needs everything this chapter covers — real navigation decisions, locators that can't just be hardcoded once and forgotten, and assertions that depend on what a specific search actually returns, not a fixed, known value.

Common Mistakes to Watch For

  • Giving an agent browser control without genuinely thinking about locator strategy — Chapter 2's tool-scoping principle applies just as much to HOW a tool finds elements as to WHETHER it has the tool at all.
  • Treating AI-driven navigation as if it needs no plan at all — this chapter's navigation page shows it's genuinely closer to Chapter 2's rough planning than to pure improvisation.
  • Trusting a screenshot analysis without understanding its real limitations — this chapter's final page is honest about what visual analysis can and can't reliably catch.

What You'll Be Able to Do

By the end of the seven pages that follow, you should be able to give an agent genuine, robust browser control, let it navigate and select elements without hardcoded assumptions, have it reason about what to assert and how confidently, watch it explore autonomously, generate real Playwright test code from what it observes, and use screenshots and traces as real, additional sources of information.
