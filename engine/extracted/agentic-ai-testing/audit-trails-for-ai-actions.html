Prerequisites: Access Control for AI Agents

What is this, and why Chapter 15's traces aren't enough

Chapter 15's traces exist to help a team DEBUG what an agent did and why — rich, detailed, optimized for engineering investigation. An audit trail answers a genuinely different question: for accountability and compliance purposes, WHO authorized WHAT action, WHEN, and under WHAT policy. These are different records with different requirements, not the same data serving two purposes.

The Genuine Gap in Chapter 15's Trace Format

Chapter 15's OWN trace format (from Tracing Agent DECISIONS):

  "run_id": "run-88213",
  "objective": "...",
  "steps": [...],
  "final_outcome": "success",
}

NOTICE what's GENUINELY absent — WHO triggered THIS run? UNDER
WHICH role (this CHAPTER'S own previous page)? WHAT specific
PERMISSION check ALLOWED it? Chapter 15's trace ANSWERS "what
did the AGENT do" — it doesn't ANSWER "who AUTHORIZED this,
and UNDER what POLICY," which is EXACTLY what accountability
GENUINELY requires.

Why These Are Two Genuinely Separate Records, Not One

Chapter 15's TRACE is OPTIMIZED for ENGINEERING investigation
— rich reasoning TEXT, step-by-STEP detail, meant to be
QUERIED and ANALYZED by a TEAM debugging AGENT behavior.

THIS page's AUDIT record is OPTIMIZED for a GENUINELY
different AUDIENCE — a COMPLIANCE reviewer or AUDITOR needing
a RELIABLE, TAMPER-resistant record of WHO did WHAT, UNDER
what AUTHORIZATION, WITHOUT needing to UNDERSTAND the agent's
FULL reasoning chain at ALL.

🧠 Think About It

Why does the audit record link to Chapter 15's trace via linked_run_id rather than duplicating the full reasoning content directly? These records serve genuinely different purposes and genuinely different audiences — an auditor reviewing authorization doesn't need the full reasoning chain, and duplicating it would bloat the audit record with information irrelevant to its actual purpose. The link preserves the ability to cross-reference into Chapter 15's richer debugging data WHEN genuinely needed, without conflating two records that should stay structurally distinct for their own separate reasons.

⚠️ Common Mistake

Assuming Chapter 15's debugging traces already constitute an adequate audit trail, since they technically record what happened. This page's own comparison shows the genuine gap — Chapter 15's format has no concept of who authorized an action or under what policy, information accountability genuinely requires.

💡 Quick Tip

Audit records should be designed assuming they may need to satisfy an external compliance reviewer who has no context on this course's internal architecture — every field should be self-explanatory on its own, unlike Chapter 15's traces which can reasonably assume an internal engineering audience.

Best Practices

  • Maintain audit trails as genuinely separate records from Chapter 15's debugging traces, optimized for accountability rather than engineering investigation.
  • Link audit records to their corresponding debugging traces rather than duplicating reasoning content, preserving cross-reference ability without conflating purposes.
  • Design audit records to be self-explanatory to an external reviewer, unlike debugging traces which can assume internal context.

🎯 Key Takeaway

  • Audit trails answer who authorized what action under what policy — genuinely different from Chapter 15's traces, which answer what the agent actually did and why.
  • Chapter 15's trace format has a genuine gap for accountability purposes — no concept of authorization, role, or policy context.
  • These should remain two separate, linked records, each optimized for its own genuinely different audience and purpose.

    """Confirms every trace has a corresponding audit record,
