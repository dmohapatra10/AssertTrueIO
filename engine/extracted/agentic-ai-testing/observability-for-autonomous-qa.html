Observability for Autonomous QA

Prerequisites: AI + CI/CD

Testing Confirms; Observability Reveals

Chapter 12 deliberately injected obstacles into controlled test pages to verify an agent's behavior. That confirms something works in the SPECIFIC scenario you tested. Observability is about a genuinely different question — what's actually happening across the hundreds of real agent runs your pipeline executes every day, continuously, including scenarios nobody deliberately constructed.   7. Debugging with Observability Data — standing data vs. Chapter 8's one-off evidence gathering
  8. Observability Best Practices — closing this chapter's threads together

A Concrete Illustration of the Gap

Chapter 12's Recovery testing DELIBERATELY injected ONE
specific obstacle (a newsletter modal) and CONFIRMED the agent
handled IT. GENUINELY valuable — but it says NOTHING about
the OBSTACLE nobody THOUGHT to test for: a THIRD-PARTY
analytics script that OCCASIONALLY hangs for 8 SECONDS,
SOMETIMES pushing a REAL production run PAST its timeout.

OBSERVABILITY is what SURFACES this — not because ANYONE
predicted it, but because STANDING, CONTINUOUS data collection
across MANY real runs REVEALS a PATTERN nobody DELIBERATELY
went looking FOR.

Common Mistakes to Watch For

  • Treating passing Chapter 12 tests as sufficient confidence for production — testing and observability answer genuinely different questions.
  • Logging everything indiscriminately — this chapter's Structured Logging page is explicit that unstructured, undifferentiated logs are nearly as unhelpful as no logs at all.
  • Alerting on every anomaly — exactly the alert-fatigue risk Chapter 8 already named, now revisited at observability scale.
